Leaves ​

The area of a leaf determines how much light it intercepts and how it exchanges heat and water. Leaf area is tedious to measure, but can be estimated from the length and width of the lamina.

Leaf area ​

The Montgomery model (Montgomery 1911) takes leaf area to be a fixed fraction of the rectangle that bounds the leaf,

A=a1LW

where L is the length and W the width of the lamina. The Montgomery parameter a1 is 0.5 for a triangular leaf and π/4≈0.785 for an elliptical one, and is between 0.57 and 0.74 for most broad-leaved plants (Shi et al. 2019). leaf_area uses a value for each plant group:

The generic value of 0.65, used for any AbstractLeafPlant without its own value:

julia
leaf_area(BroadleafPlant(), 10.0u"cm", 5.0u"cm")
32.5 cm^2

The area has the units of the product of the length and width:

julia
leaf_area(BroadleafPlant(), 0.1u"m", 50.0u"mm")
3.25 mm m

The parameter itself is the coefficient of the MontgomeryLaw:

julia
[(nameof(typeof(taxon)), montgomery_law(LeafArea(), taxon).coefficient)
 for taxon in (BroadleafPlant(), Bambusoideae(), Liriodendron(), Rosaceae(), Lauraceae(), Oleaceae())]
6-element Vector{Tuple{Symbol, Float64}}:
 (:BroadleafPlant, 0.65)
 (:Bambusoideae, 0.74)
 (:Liriodendron, 0.73)
 (:Rosaceae, 0.62)
 (:Lauraceae, 0.67)
 (:Oleaceae, 0.7)

Leaf dry mass ​

Leaf dry mass scales with leaf area to the power 1.10, less than the 1.5 expected if leaves kept their shape, because larger leaves are relatively thinner (Milla and Reich 2007). leaf_dry_mass takes either the area, or the length and width:

julia
leaf_dry_mass(BroadleafPlant(), 20.0u"cm^2")
0.26985656953471276 g

The leaf mass per area, a key trait of the leaf economics spectrum, therefore rises slowly with leaf size:

julia
areas = exp10.(range(0, 3; length = 50)) .* u"cm^2"
fig, ax = figure_axis("Leaf area (cm²)", "Leaf mass per area (g/m²)"; xscale = log10)
lines!(ax, ustrip.(u"cm^2", areas), ustrip.(u"g/m^2", leaf_dry_mass.(Ref(BroadleafPlant()), areas) ./ areas);
       linewidth = 2)
fig
