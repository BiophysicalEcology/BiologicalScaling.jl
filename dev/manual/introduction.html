Introduction ​

BiologicalScaling.jl provides empirical equations relating biological traits to body size: metabolic rate, morphology, locomotion, cardiorespiratory physiology, life history and leaf dimensions, and classical theories of how the proportions of limbs must change with size.

Allometry ​

Allometry is the study of how traits change with body size. Most allometric relationships are power laws,

y=aMb

where M is body mass, a the coefficient and b the exponent. On logarithmic axes a power law is a straight line with slope b. When b is the value expected if shape is unchanged, 1 for masses and volumes, 2/3 for areas and 1/3 for lengths, the scaling is isometric; otherwise it is allometric in the narrow sense. Metabolic rate, for example, scales with an exponent near 3/4 rather than 1, so larger animals use less energy per unit mass (Kleiber 1932):

julia
[basal_metabolic_rate(EutherianMammal(), M) / M for M in [0.02, 2.0, 200.0] .* u"kg"]
3-element Vector{Quantity{Float64, 𝐋^2 𝐓^-3, Unitful.FreeUnits{(kg^-1, W), 𝐋^2 𝐓^-3, nothing}}}:
 8.881554147898129 W kg^-1
 2.8085940269474063 W kg^-1
 0.888155414789813 W kg^-1

In this package, allometric means an empirical equation fitted to comparative data, as opposed to a mechanistic equation derived from physical principles, such as the heat budgets of HeatExchange.jl.

Traits and taxa ​

Every prediction dispatches on the trait, a subtype of AbstractScalingVariable, and the taxon, a subtype of AbstractTaxon:

julia
allometric(variable, taxon, inputs...)

The traits and taxa are singleton types, such as BasalMetabolicRate() and EutherianMammal(). The taxa form a hierarchy, so an equation for AbstractMammal applies to all mammals, and one for a particular group, such as Primate, overrides it:

julia
brain_mass(EutherianMammal(), 5.0u"kg")
0.030851693136000478 kg

A combination of trait and taxon with no equation is a MethodError, rather than a silent extrapolation from another group. allometric_inputs gives the inputs each combination needs:

julia
allometric_inputs(BasalMetabolicRate(), EutherianMammal()), allometric_inputs(StandardMetabolicRate(), Squamate()),
allometric_inputs(LeafArea(), BroadleafPlant())
((:mass,), (:mass, :temperature), (:length, :width))

Each trait also has a named function, such as basal_metabolic_rate or leaf_area, that calls allometric, and trait_name gives the standard name of a trait in traits.build databases:

julia
trait_name(BasalMetabolicRate())
"metabolic_rate_basal"

Equations as objects ​

The equations are held as objects that carry their units and the reference they come from. A PowerLaw is a univariate power law, and a MontgomeryLaw gives leaf area from leaf length and width:

julia
kleiber = power_law(BasalMetabolicRate(), EutherianMammal())
PowerLaw{Float64, Float64, Unitful.FreeUnits{(kg,), 𝐌, nothing}, Unitful.FreeUnits{(W,), 𝐋^2 𝐌 𝐓^-3, nothing}}(3.34, 0.75, kg, W, "Schmidt-Nielsen 1975 J. Exp. Zool. 194:287–307; McNab 2008 Comp. Biochem. Physiol. A 151:5–28")

Both are callable, and reference gives the citation:

julia
kleiber(70.0u"kg"), montgomery(10.0u"cm", 5.0u"cm"), reference(kleiber)
(80.82951944928581 W, 32.5 cm^2, "Schmidt-Nielsen 1975 J. Exp. Zool. 194:287–307; McNab 2008 Comp. Biochem. Physiol. A 151:5–28")

New equations can be built in the same way, here with made-up values:

julia
amphibian_smr = PowerLaw(0.0033, 0.84; input_unit = u"g", output_unit = u"W",
                         reference = "Illustrative values only")
amphibian_smr(10.0u"g")
0.022830422040324903 W

Units ​

All inputs and outputs are Unitful.jl quantities. Each equation is defined in the units of its source, grams for some and kilograms for others, and converts whatever is passed:

julia
skin_area(PasserineBird(), 0.02u"kg"), skin_area(PasserineBird(), 20.0u"g")
(73.75424253432696 cm^2, 73.75424253432696 cm^2)

BiologicalScaling.jl in the BiophysicalEcology ecosystem ​

BiologicalScaling.jl can be used on its own, and is also part of the BiophysicalEcology packages for mechanistic niche modelling, to be brought together in NicheMapper.jl (in development). There it provides the sizes, shapes and rates of organisms that other packages need:

PackageWhat BiologicalScaling.jl can provide
ThermalPhysiology.jlMetabolic rates at a reference temperature, corrected for body temperature with Arrhenius and thermal performance models
HeatExchange.jlSurface, skin, plumage and silhouette areas for the exchange of heat by radiation, convection and evaporation
BiophysicalGeometry.jlLimb aspect ratios and body-part proportions for the shapes of composite bodies
AnimalMapper.jl (in development)Energy and water budgets of animals over time and space

The ThermalPhysiology.jl tutorial on metabolic rate combines the two packages.
